Run #52 – The Final Half: One Perfect Loop Through San Francisco

Tagline: Golden Gate Park, the Presidio, the Golden Gate Bridge, Ocean Beach — a blue-sky victory lap, and 52 halves in 52 weeks, done.

For the final run of the 52@52 challenge, Paula and I went back to Golden Gate Park — one of our favorite places among all the spots this challenge took us to. But we didn't stay only in the park: we started and finished there, and in between covered a beautiful loop through the northwest of the city.

The route: park, bridge, museums, and ocean

Heading north out of Golden Gate Park, we ran through the Presidio area, crossed to the west side close to the Golden Gate Bridge, continued past the Legion of Honor museum — with views of the west beaches from the road — and then went down to Ocean Beach before heading back to the park.

This route came from the RunGo app (it's called "Legion of Honor + Presidio"), and I recommend it. The final numbers: 13.2 miles and 1,036 feet of elevation in 2 hours and 24 minutes. A worthy final half marathon.

The crew, one last time

Paula rode her bike alongside me the whole way — as she has done for so many of these runs. And David joined us at the Legion of Honor, running with me to the end. Finishing with my loving wife and a great friend.

No audiobook, just the views

I didn't listen to any audiobook this time. I really wanted to pay attention to the path and the views — and the day made it easy. With a perfect blue sky and amazing visibility, we had great views of the bay, the Golden Gate Bridge, and the ocean. An amazing way to finish the 52@52 challenge!

So... is this really the end?

Well, it's the end of the original challenge: I achieved my goal of running 52 half marathons in the 52 weeks of the year I was 52 years old. Next week I'm turning 53.

And that's it, no more challenges after that? No spoilers. You will have to wait until next weekend to find out.

The whole challenge in one word: consistency

So, to summarize the whole thing, I would like to use one word: consistency! That was the most difficult thing of this challenge, but at the same time it was the most rewarding thing too.

The difficult part

Yeah, some days I didn't feel like running a half-marathon, but I went out and did it anyway. Some days were harder than others, some days I had company (most of the time, thanks to my amazing wife Paula!), some days I was alone. But still, I completed the challenge! One weekend I missed the run since I was sick, then during the week I caught up.

The rewarding part

So you got the difficulty aspect of it, but where is the rewarding side? Well, as people say (at least I do!), everything you do every day changes your life. If you help people every day, you become a better person. If you say good things about your day and yourself every day, you become happier. If you try to eat healthy every day, your body improves. Same with exercise — although most people are not really ready to do heavy exercises every day, at least I'm not. So I decided to do a half-marathon per week. To me, a big challenge, but once a week is still consistency — and going the entire year like that really improved my capacity to do other things consistently, to face challenges, and now I can go through them even if they look scary and hard at the beginning. So yeah, it is rewarding!

The numbers

Of course, a total of 1,127.4 km / 700.53 miles from all these 52 half-marathons, plus 28,311.05 ft / 8,629 m of elevation, is impressive — at least for me. In my entire life — 51 years — before this challenge, I had done no more than 30 half-marathons, plus one marathon, and one "ultra" of 50.6 km. But most impressive to me is the consistency!

Pick your challenge

So pick your challenge, set your consistency goal, and do it! Whatever it is, if it's something you believe is going to make you better, or make others around you better, go for it! And good luck.
