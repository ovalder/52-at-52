Run #51 – A First Look at Lake Chabot, One Run from the Finish

Tagline: Morning light, lake views, a revised race plan, and a preview of November’s challenge — only one to go.

Run number 51 brought me back home after vacation and a few days sick with COVID-19. Yes, I actually tested — I still had an old test at home. This time, I headed somewhere new: Lake Chabot, the same place where I’ll be racing on November 7.

And what a discovery! I came to get familiar with the location and left with a new favorite place to run.

A change of plans for November

While I was sick and missing both the gym and my shorter runs, I reconsidered the 50K registration. Taking on that distance just a couple of weeks after being sick felt like too much, so I changed to the 30K.

That also means I’ll have a better chance of seeing my friends who are running the half marathon. For the 50K, I figured I would need at least seven hours — and I’m not sure anyone would still be waiting for me by then! Thirty kilometers is still a challenge, just a more reasonable one for where I am right now.

Morning light, lake views, and a glimpse of the bay

I started at 8:30 a.m., with the temperature around 11°C and the sun still low enough to give the scenery some beautiful morning light. There were plenty of opportunities for photos, especially along the lake.

As the trail climbed, the views opened up beyond the water to the city and even the bay. Between the weather, the light, and the changing scenery, this was a very nice welcome home after the Hawaiian runs.

Elevation without the obstacle course

The trail close to the lake was paved, while the rest of the route I explored was mostly firm, packed dirt — easy to run on, without the rocks and tree roots that kept me watching every step at Quicksilver. The path felt open, and much of it had plenty of shade.

For the sections I ran, this felt close to an ideal introduction to trail running: some elevation, but non-technical terrain where I could relax and enjoy the surroundings. My Altra Experience Wild 2 felt perfect for it.

I’d happily recommend this route to someone looking for hills without an obstacle course. For a bigger challenge in both climbing and footing, Quicksilver is still waiting — my runs #45 and #47 can tell you all about that!

A familiar audiobook in a new setting

I continued listening to Bag of Bones by Stephen King through Chirp. I think I’m getting close to the end now, and the story has become really exciting. A good audiobook, a beautiful morning, and a new trail to explore made a pretty good combination.

Run #51 in the books. A revised plan for November, a new place worth returning to, and only one half marathon left in the 52@52 challenge.

See you next time!
