Run #43 – First Official Race: 25K by the Sea at UMS

Beach sand, river paths, and one very lucky bottle of cold water.

Run number 43 broke new ground for the 52@52 project: for the first time, one of the half marathons was completed inside an official event race—the 25K distance of UMS – Ultramaratona Sergipe, an ambitious gathering in Aracaju that also staged 50K and 100K races and brought in runners from several Brazilian states.

A rare weekend at home, put to perfect use

This was the only weekend I would be in Aracaju for a while, so having a real event to run instead of another solo half marathon felt like a small gift from the calendar. Toeing a start line again, surrounded by runners who had traveled from all over the country for the 50K and 100K distances too, gave the whole morning a different kind of energy from the usual self-organized runs.

The route: ocean, river, and the beach finish

The 25K course turned out to be one of the best routes of the whole 52@52 challenge so far. It hugged the ocean, ran alongside the Sergipe River, mixed paved stretches with unpaved sandy paths, and then closed with a finishing stretch straight over the beach sand. Aracaju’s beaches are almost custom-built for this kind of finish: a wide, flat, compacted strip of sand that runs firm underfoot instead of the loose, ankle-twisting sand you might expect. Finishing a half marathon with the ocean on one side and that kind of surface under the feet is hard to beat.

The result: 6th in category, despite the heat

The performance was solid. Even with the heat and humidity working against every runner on the course, I crossed the line in 6th place in my age category (50 to 59). The distance ran a bit long, as event courses often do—official 25K, but my Pixel Watch 4 recorded about 24.5K, which tracks with how these things usually shake out on race day.

A smart touch: the WhatsApp channel

One detail from the organization deserves its own callout. Before the race, UMS set up a WhatsApp channel for each distance, with only the organizers able to post. It became the go-to spot for race-day logistics, updates, and reminders, all in one place instead of digging through emails.

After the race, they opened that same channel so all participants could chat among themselves—sharing opinions about the race, links to photos and recap posts, tips on upcoming races, and even products they use and recommend. It turned the channel into a lasting hub where finishers could connect with each other after the event.

One suggestion for race organizers: use two channels from the start—one strictly for official announcements and a separate participant discussion space—rather than converting the same channel after the race.

I had never seen this approach at any race I ran in the United States, and this was the first time I saw it in Brazil too. I hope more organizers adopt it, because it works very well.

The water story: lucky me, unlucky many

The most memorable part of the day came after the finish, inside that same WhatsApp channel for the 25K runners. It turned out I had been lucky: I found water at every hydration station along the course, mostly at room temperature but with a few very welcome cold cups mixed in.

A lot of other runners were not so fortunate. Several stations ran dry, and plenty of people had to make an unplanned stop at a grocery store along the route just to buy something—anything—to drink. Even that backup plan failed for many, since the store itself ran out of both water and soda before the field had passed through. It is a rough experience to hear about after a hot, humid 25K, and I hope UMS takes the lesson into next year’s edition: hydration on course is not optional in this climate.

Gel report: one no, one reliable yes

Race-kit gels are always a gamble, and this one confirmed it. I tried the DURAGEL Nitro400 that came with the race kit, and it was not a great experience: the taste was off, and it had little crystals—probably sugar—that felt unpleasant in the mouth while trying to chew through it. On the plus side, it was easy to swallow. Overall verdict: a firm no for me; I could not even finish the packet.

I also brought a couple of Huma gels as backup, and the Mango Huma delivered exactly what I have come to expect from it—smooth and pleasant, no complaints. I never even got around to opening the second Huma gel I carried, which says a lot about how comfortable the effort felt.

Legs, knees, and no audiobook

Despite the longer-than-advertised distance and the typical northeastern Brazilian “winter” heat—which, as always, is not particularly cold—the legs and knees stayed quiet the whole way. No complaints during the run, just the expected tiredness the next day and nothing more.

For this one, I skipped the audiobook entirely, choosing instead to stay fully immersed in the event atmosphere and take in the views along the ocean and the river—a decision that paid off given how good this route was.

Run #43 down. A new format unlocked for the 52@52 challenge, a course worth remembering, and a good reminder to always carry a backup plan for hydration on race day.
