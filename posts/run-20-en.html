Run #20 – Best Crew Ever on the Coyote Creek Trail

📅 2026-03-29


Shaking off a missed week

Run #20 came after an unplanned break: I had to skip last week’s run because of a cold and sore throat, so I knew this one wasn’t going to be easy. I’m still recovering, and it showed—I had to walk a few times along the way just to keep things under control—but at least my knees and legs behaved, with no real pain during the run.

Best crew ever: Paula and Laura

To balance out the rough physical side, I had the best possible company: my wife, Paula, and my daughter, Laura, came along on their bikes on the Coyote Creek Trail. Even though I’m not exactly able to chat much while I’m running, just having them riding nearby, checking on me, and being part of the outing turned what could have been a lonely grind into something that felt shared and special. Laura even carried my water bottle on her bike, which was a lifesaver on this hot day.

Hydration, heat, and a bit of spoiling

The heat made a big difference this time. When it’s warm, the water in my running backpack heats up pretty quickly, and drinking “tea‑temperature” water mid‑run is never fun. Having a proper bottle of cold water on Laura’s bike meant I could stop, take a few real gulps, and cool down better than usual. I definitely got spoiled with this setup—on the next runs, I’m already planning to ask Paula to carry the bottle for me again.

A great trail with one uneasy stretch

The Coyote Creek Trail is really nice overall, especially for long, flat efforts like this challenge demands. There is, however, one stretch a little north of the Los Lagos Golf Course where several homeless people have set up camp, and they keep a number of loose dogs (and even some chickens) around. I’ve never had any direct problems passing through there, but it’s always a bit uneasy to run by, mostly because of the unleashed dogs and the unpredictability of the situation. For the next runs in this area, I’ll probably change my starting point to Hellyer County Park and head south from there, avoiding that section altogether while still enjoying one of my favorite local trails.
