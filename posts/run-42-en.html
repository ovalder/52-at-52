Run #42: A Solo Loop Around Pampulha, Jet Lag and All

Jet-lagged, not stopped: Lap two around Pampulha's postcard shoreline.

Some runs happen because you're inspired. This one happened because I refused to let a missed weekend turn into a missed week — jet-lagged, tired from the trip to Brazil, and honestly not feeling it, I laced up anyway and got Run #42 of the 52@52 challenge done solo around Pampulha Lake in Belo Horizonte.

This marks the second time this challenge has brought me back to this exact loop, and it did not disappoint. With 41 halves already logged and the finish line of the challenge getting closer, skipping a session simply wasn't an option, even on a day my body was voting for the couch.

The Route: Pampulha's Paved Perimeter

The path circling Pampulha Lake is one of Belo Horizonte's best-known running and cycling routes, tracing the shoreline of the largest artificial lake in the city. It's fully paved, flat, and threads through a landscape that mixes nature with some of Brazil's most significant modernist architecture.

Along the way, the route passes several landmarks that make this loop feel more like an open-air museum than a training run:

- Pampulha Art Museum, the former casino turned contemporary art space - Casa do Baile (the "Ballroom Party House"), a graceful concrete ribbon hugging the water - The Pampulha Church (Igreja São Francisco de Assis), designed by Oscar Niemeyer with tile murals by Cândido Portinari, part of the UNESCO-listed Pampulha Modern Ensemble - Estádio Mineirão and Arena Mineirinho, the city's iconic football stadium and arena - The Pampulha Yacht Club, with its lakeside terraces and skyline reflections

The entire ensemble was conceived in the 1940s as a cultural and leisure center around the lake, a project that put Niemeyer on the map before Brasília. Running past these buildings mid-effort is a reminder that this route is as much a cultural tour as it is a training loop.

Conditions and How the Run Felt

The weather cooperated fully: sunny skies, 19 degrees Celsius, and a steady breeze that kept core temperature in check for the whole distance. Running solo gave space to just take in the lake, the architecture, and the quiet satisfaction of turning a "no way I'm running today" morning into a completed half.

Jet lag has a way of making even familiar routes feel longer, but the flat, scenic paving around Pampulha made the miles pass easily despite the fatigue.
