Run #44 – Trails, Elevation, and a Fort Knox of a Water Tank at Alum Rock Park

Tagline: California's oldest municipal park, one very well-guarded water tank, and a solid comeback pace.

Run number 44 stayed close to home in the Bay Area, trading beach sand for dirt trails and hills at Alum Rock Park — a gem tucked into the foothills east of San Jose that happens to be California's oldest municipal park, dating back to 1872.

A local classic for trail time and elevation

Alum Rock Park is only a short trip from home, which makes it a go-to whenever the plan calls for trail time and some real elevation instead of another flat road loop. The park is refreshingly flexible about how you use it: there are miles of off-road singletrack and fire roads for a proper trail effort, but there is also a paved option running alongside Penitencia Creek, which is what I usually stick to when I come out here on the bike. This time, though, it was all about legs and lungs, not wheels.

The numbers: 13.2 miles, 360m of climbing, 2:32:09

This one counted as a good race back home, especially considering I had just come back from a trip and hadn't had much time to reset before lacing up. Covering 13.2 miles with about 360m of elevation gain in 2:32:09 on a trail course with real climbs is a solid result under those conditions — the kind of pace that confirms the legs remember what to do even after time away, hills and all.

The best-guarded water tank in the Bay Area

Almost at the end of the run, I passed by a public utility facility that looked like a water tank sitting at ground level, tucked behind a fence. What actually caught my attention wasn't the tank itself, but the sheer number of locks stacked on the gates. Someone clearly had a generous budget allocated specifically for locks — the kind of detail you only notice when you've got 13 miles and 360m of climbing in your legs and plenty of time to appreciate the small, strange things along the trail.

Run #44 done. Good trails, a decent comeback pace after travel, and a new record for most locks spotted on a single gate.
