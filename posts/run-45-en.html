Run #45 – A Reality Check on the Rocks and Roots of Almaden Quicksilver

Tagline: Narrow trails, tired legs, near-falls, and a hard truth about a 50K signed up too soon.

Run number 45 was all about training with a purpose. I had just registered for the Lake Chabot 50K trail run on November 7th, a distance I'm honestly nowhere near ready for, especially with roughly 1,000m of elevation gain packed into the course. So the plan for this one was simple: chase more elevation, and Almaden Quicksilver County Park was the obvious answer.

Narrow trails, constant ups and downs

Almaden Quicksilver has become one of my favorite local spots for exactly this kind of training. I love the narrow trail paths and the way the terrain never lets you settle into a rhythm — it's constant up and down, hill after hill. The one real hazard is the amount of rocks and tree roots scattered across the singletrack. If you're not watching the ground closely, and especially if fatigue is creeping in, it's very easy to trip. I was doing both at the same time — tired and not watching closely enough — and I nearly went down a few times. Small mercy: I wasn't holding my 360-degree camera this time, unlike at Angel Island, where I actually tripped and hit the ground while filming.

Fun trail, tough day

Despite the close calls, this trail is genuinely fun. There are some really nice views along the way, the steep sections stay quiet with very few people around, and the run finishes with a great overlook of the Guadalupe Reservoir. But it was a tough outing overall — tougher and warmer than I expected, which forced me to walk far more than planned. In the end, I covered 14.5 miles with 865m of elevation gain in 3:35:15, a long time out there by any measure. Still, 865m is noticeably less than the roughly 1,000m waiting for me at Lake Chabot, so there's clearly more climbing ahead to get used to.

Gear that held up: Huma, water, and the Altra Wild 2

I was glad to have water and two Huma gels with me to get through the heat and the extra walking. On the shoe front, I ran again in my Altra Wild 2, and it delivered once more. Even after kicking rocks and clipping tree roots more than once, my toenails were completely fine at the end — no bruising, no pain. I think the runner's lace loop deserves some credit too, keeping my feet locked in place and stopping them from sliding forward on the downhills, which is usually where toenails take the worst beating.

The honest truth about signing up for 50K

I'll need to come back here several more times to get my shape — and my legs — ready for the extra elevation waiting at Lake Chabot. Honestly, if I hadn't already registered, this run alone might have talked me out of being so ambitious — a half marathon, like two friends of mine already signed up for, would have sounded a lot more reasonable right about now. But the registration is done, so the training starts here.

Run #45 in the books. Rough terrain, a rough pace, 865m of climbing under the legs, and a very clear signal that the real work for November starts now.

See you next time!
