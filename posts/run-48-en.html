Run #48 – Back Where It All Began, Then All the Way to 21.9 Miles

Tagline: A first-run starting point, a 30K training target, October heat, and five minutes of walk-in-fridge recovery.

Run number 48 was not just another half marathon. With the Lake Chabot 50K trail run on November 7 getting closer, I needed a longer training day — at least 30K, according to the plan my Google Health AI advisor built for me. For a run where I needed to be able to add distance as needed, I went to the place I knew could deliver: the southern portion of the Coyote Creek Trail.

And I started right where the whole 52@52 journey began: the parking lot at the Tully Community Branch Library on Tully Road.

Back to the beginning, with a much bigger number in mind

There was something fitting about starting this run at the same point as Run #1. Back then, the goal was simply to begin a challenge that sounded a little crazy: 52 half marathons in 52 weeks. This time, the mission was to build toward something even bigger. The Coyote Creek Trail was the practical choice — long, almost flat, and flexible enough to turn around whenever the distance target was met. The southern reach begins at Tully Road and runs south through Santa Clara County toward Morgan Hill, making it ideal for this kind of long out-and-back effort [web:72][web:82].

By the end, I had covered 21.9 miles — about 35.2 km — comfortably beyond the 30K target and a meaningful long-run step toward Lake Chabot.

Heat advisory, early start

It was a hot day for October. The Bay Area was under a Heat Advisory, with the National Weather Service urging people to shift strenuous outdoor activity to cooler hours and stay hydrated [web:73]. Starting at 7:30 a.m. made a real difference: it was about 19°C / 66°F when I set off, which was manageable before the day heated up.

Coyote Creek does not have anything close to the shade of Quicksilver, but elevation would have made this kind of distance much harder. For this training day, the nearly flat route was the right compromise: less shade, but enough runnable trail to keep going for as long as the plan required.

A very cold gas-station break

On the return, around mile 15.5, I stopped at a gas station close to the trail for something cold to drink. I picked up a Gatorade, then took full advantage of the beer-and-wine walk-in fridge in the corner of the store to cool down for a few minutes.

It was funny to see not only my breath turn visible in the cold air — like it does on an actual cold day — but also a mist of vapor rising from my warm, sweaty body. Five minutes inside that fridge felt surprisingly great. A very improvised recovery station, but exactly what I needed at that point.

Still listening, still building

I kept listening to Bag of Bones through Chirp. The story is getting genuinely interesting now, although I still have about a quarter of the audiobook left. That gave me plenty to listen to on a long, mostly flat route where the miles can otherwise start blending together.

Run #48 done. Back at the original starting point, 21.9 miles in the legs, more than 30K checked off, and one long step closer to Lake Chabot.

See you next time!
