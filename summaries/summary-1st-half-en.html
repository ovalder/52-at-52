52@52 – Halfway There: A Look Back at the First 26 Runs

📅 2026-05-03

How it all started

The 52@52 challenge was born from a spontaneous decision at a 50K trail race: turn the year of my 52nd birthday into a journey of 52 half-marathons in 52 weeks. What started as a wild idea quickly became a commitment, a blog, a YouTube channel, and a story worth telling in chapters. Twenty-six runs later, I've officially crossed the halfway line — and I'm still standing, still running, and still writing.

The places I've run

In 26 weeks, the challenge has taken me across an impressive range of terrain and geography. The Bay Area trails have been my home base: the flat, creek-hugging Coyote Creek Trail, the clean Stevens Creek Trail, the northeast Bay's Iron Horse Trail, the Penitencia Creek Trail all the way to Alum Rock Park, the iconic Golden Gate Park with ocean views, the SF Bay trails around Coyote Point, and the Los Gatos Creek Trail. But the challenge has also gone far beyond the Bay Area: a muddy race at Rancho San Vicente, the Pleasanton Muddy Dash obstacle course, a point-to-point to Google Café in Mountain View, a sightseeing run through the streets of Paris, a beach run in Maragogi (Brazil), a charity run in Aracaju for the manatees, a loop around Pampulha Lake in Belo Horizonte, and a treadmill run on the deck of a cruise ship somewhere between Mexico and Los Angeles. Run #25 even became a proper commute run, leaving home on foot and finishing at the office.

The people who made it better

Paula, my wife, has been on her bike alongside me for a large part of these runs — crew, photographer, videographer, and cold-water carrier all in one. Her Meta Oakley smart glasses have captured footage I couldn't have gotten any other way. My daughter Laura joined us on a couple of Coyote Creek runs — "best crew ever" is not an exaggeration. Friends like Marco, Marcelo, Herminio, Derek, Sandro, Yves, and Carl have joined for different chapters, and Yves even set a personal record during Run #13 at Golden Gate Park.

The body's report card

This has been as much a story about listening to the body as it has been about logging distance. The right knee was the main character in the early drama, shouting on some runs and behaving almost perfectly on others. The left calf made recurring guest appearances. Hip mobility work, targeted stretching, less sitting, and shoe rotation have all made a real difference. By the second quarter the trend line was clearly pointing in the right direction: less pain, more confidence, and zero injuries serious enough to stop the streak.

The shoes

The challenge has been an unofficial shoe review series. I started in New Balance More Trail v3 trail shoes and Hoka Clifton 8s, moved through the Topo Athletic Phantom 4, spent a long stretch with the Altra Rivera 3 as I leaned into a lower-drop philosophy (inspired by Born to Run), and debuted the Altra Escalante 4 in the second quarter. Finding the right shoe for the right day remains part of the ongoing experiment.

Fuel and gadgets

Huma gels have been the consistent race-day fuel, and Nuun Sport tablets in a cold water bottle — carried by Paula on the bike rather than in the backpack bladder — have made hot-weather runs much more manageable. UCAN Edge made one appearance and earned the permanent nickname "rebel gel." On the tech side: the Insta360 X5, Meta Oakley smart glasses, a Google Pixel Watch 4, and a Garmin Venu 2 Plus, sometimes both on the same wrists at the same time.

The audiobook shelf

Books have been a parallel journey through the miles. Here is what kept me company, and when:

  • NOS4A2 – Joe Hill → Runs 4, 5, and finishing in Run 7. My first horror audiobook, and a great introduction to Joe Hill's style.
  • End Game – Jeffrey Archer → Started in Run 7, continued through Runs 8 (would have, but forgot headphones!), and finished in Run 14. A political thriller that stretched across many weeks.
  • Born to Run – Christopher McDougall → Runs 9 and 10. Listened on the cruise ship treadmill and back on the creeks at home — and it directly influenced my return to lower-drop shoes.
  • Project Hail Mary – Andy Weir → Runs 17 and 18. From the shores of Pampulha Lake in Brazil to the beach in Maragogi, this one was perfect company in the heat.
  • Dark Matter – Blake Crouch → Started in Run 20 and finished in Run 23. Just as disorienting and gripping as the protagonist.
  • All Systems Red (The Murderbot Diaries #1) – Martha Wells → Finished in Run 24, on the Penitencia Creek Trail to Alum Rock. Murderbot's sarcastic voice was perfect company for a solo run in the rain.
  • Ready Player Two – Ernest Cline → Started during Run 26. The sequel to one of the books I've loved most in my life, and a fitting way to start the second half of the challenge.

Several runs had no audiobook at all — either by necessity (new route, forgotten headphones, noisy urban commute) or by choice (Paris sightseeing, beach in Aracaju, soaking in Brazil).

Distance summary

Total after 26 runs: 343.02 miles / 552.04 km

The biggest milestones

  • Run #9: On a cruise ship treadmill — probably the most unusual venue in the challenge.
  • Run #11: Golden Gate Park with almost no knee pain — the turning point.
  • Run #13: Friends, a park, and Yves setting a half-marathon personal record.
  • Run #16: A solo sightseeing run through the streets of Paris.
  • Runs #17–19: Brazil — Belo Horizonte, Maragogi beach, Aracaju.
  • Run #25: First true "run commute" — left home on foot, arrived at the office.
  • Week of Runs 24–25–26: Three half-marathons in one week. 39.96 miles / 64.3 km — the most I've ever run in a single week, beating even the 50K race week from last October.
  • Run #26: The halfway point. 26 down, 26 to go. No major injuries. Still going.

What the first half really says

The first 26 runs have been less about the distance and more about building something durable. I've run in mud, sand, asphalt, treadmill belts, trail gravel, and Parisian streets. I've run sick, tired, jet-lagged, overheated, and occasionally very happy. I've learned more about my body, my shoes, my hydration, my form, and my limits in six months of half-marathons than in years of more casual running. And I've kept the streak alive without a single permanent gap. Three hundred and forty-three miles down. Another three hundred and forty to go. See you at the finish line in November.
