52@52 – The Year of the Halves: The Full Story

📅 2026-11-01

On November 1, 2026, I ran the 52nd and final half marathon of the challenge — a victory lap from Golden Gate Park through the Presidio, past the Golden Gate Bridge and the Legion of Honor, down to Ocean Beach and back. Fifty-two half marathons in 52 weeks, during the year I was 52 years old. Done.

One word: consistency

So, to summarize the whole thing, I would like to use one word: consistency!

That was the most difficult thing of this challenge, but at the same time it was the most rewarding thing too.

The difficult part

Yeah, some days I didn't feel like running a half-marathon, but I went out and did it anyway. Some days were harder than others, some days I had company (most of the time, thanks to my amazing wife Paula!), some days I was alone. But still, I completed the challenge! One weekend I missed the run since I was sick, then during the week I caught up.

The rewarding part

So you got the difficulty aspect of it, but where is the rewarding side?

Well, as people say (at least I do!), everything you do every day changes your life. If you help people every day, you become a better person. If you say good things about your day and yourself every day, you become happier. If you try to eat healthy every day, your body improves. Same with exercise — although most people are not really ready to do heavy exercises every day, at least I'm not. So I decided to do a half-marathon per week. To me, a big challenge, but once a week is still consistency — and going the entire year like that really improved my capacity to do other things consistently, to face challenges, and now I can go through them even if they look scary and hard at the beginning. So yeah, it is rewarding!

The year in places and people

The challenge started at Coyote Creek, on the flat Tully Road stretch, back in November 2025 — and the Bay Area remained home base the whole year: Golden Gate Park, the Los Gatos Creek and Stevens Creek trails, the Iron Horse Trail, Penitencia Creek out to Alum Rock Park, Coyote Point by the bay, the narrow up-and-down paths of Quicksilver, Angel Island (three times!), and a first look at Lake Chabot right before the finale.

But the challenge also went far beyond: a treadmill on a cruise ship deck, a sightseeing run through the streets of Paris, Brazil more than once (Belo Horizonte's Pampulha Lake, the beach in Maragogi, and the UMS 25K race in Aracaju — the first official race of the challenge, where I finished 6th in my category), and the Hawaiian islands of Maui and Kauai, with ocean-spray finishes over lava rocks.

Paula was on her bike alongside me for a large part of these runs — crew, photographer, and cold-water carrier all in one. My daughter Laura joined the crew on Coyote Creek, and David ran the final stretch of Run #52 with me. Friends like Marco, Marcelo, Herminio, Derek, Sandro, Yves, and Carl showed up along the way, turning many of these halves into shared stories.

The numbers

Of course, a total of 1,127.4 km / 700.53 miles from all these 52 half-marathons, plus 28,311.05 ft / 8,629 m of elevation, is impressive — at least for me. In my entire life — 51 years — before this challenge, I had done no more than 30 half-marathons, plus one marathon, and one "ultra" of 50.6 km.

But most impressive to me is the consistency!

Pick your challenge

So pick your challenge, set your consistency goal, and do it! Whatever it is, if it's something you believe is going to make you better, or make others around you better, go for it! And good luck.
